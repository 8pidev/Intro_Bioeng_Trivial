# Course question bank

240 original English-language multiple-choice questions for first-year students.
Each category contains 24 introductory concepts, 12 applications, and 4 challenges.
The aim is conceptual confidence, familiar examples, and short calculations, not
memorization of equipment brands, historical dates, or advanced derivations.

## Local teaching sources

- Intro_Bioeng_Optical_Imaging_v2.pdf: generation of light, lenses and numerical
  aperture, magnification/resolution, scattering, fluorescence filtering,
  microscopy, optical tomography, and tissue contrast.
- Intro_Bioeng_Overview.pdf: energy conservation and light-tissue interactions;
  ELISA and FACS are covered in the latter section (pages 50-71).
- Digital_Image_Basics.pdf: pixels, sampling, field of view, quantization,
  bit depth, contrast, LUTs, and ImageJ interpretation.
- MedicalImaging.pdf: projections/tomography, X-ray/CT, nuclear medicine, MRI,
  ultrasound, and contrast mechanisms.
- Intro_Bioeng_Neuroimaging.pptx: EEG and hemodynamic brain imaging, hemoglobin,
  optical reconstruction, and forward/inverse problems at a conceptual level.
- optical_imaging_milk_practice_theory.pdf and
  optical_system_spatial_resolution_review.pdf: scattering experiment, PSF,
  FWHM, and saturation.
- The Moodle XML exam export in this directory was inspected for topic scope and
  expected reasoning. The new questions do not reproduce its question wording.

## Design choices

- Blue: everyday physics and units; no advanced wave mathematics.
- Pink: absorption/scattering/fluorescence and familiar biological examples.
- Yellow: image formation and microscope components.
- Purple: interpretation and image quality, with modest arithmetic.
- Green: modality principles, complementary information, and brain imaging.
- Orange: antibody recognition, assay controls and calibration, and cell sorting.

Some ideas intentionally recur in different contexts to connect the course:
fluorescence links microscopy and FACS; absorption links tissue optics and ELISA;
resolution links lenses, digital sampling, and the milk experiment.

Answers are shuffled when drawn, while the correct answer is tracked. Difficulty
labels are hidden metadata. All teams share the same non-repeating category pools.
The 60/30/10 split describes the bank, not a quota enforced in every session.
Each question text is used at most once per game across all teams and categories.
An exhausted category offers the remaining categories or a skipped turn.
